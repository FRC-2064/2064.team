## What build season is

Build season is the design-and-build window after kickoff. Kickoff is typically the first Saturday in January, when school returns from winter break. *FIRST* reveals the game that morning. Every team in the world reads the same manual at the same time.

Team 2064 then has about six weeks to turn that manual into a robot that can play. The deadline is stop-build day, set by *FIRST* before the first event. After that date the competition robot is frozen for the event it is entered in. Practice, pit fixes, and the next event still happen. A new mechanism invented after stop build usually does not.

Fall meetings, XRP projects, and last year's robot are how you learn the shop. Build season is when the game is real and the clock is short.

---

## What those six weeks are for

This is the same loop as the design-process module, dated.

| When | What the team is doing | What a new member should notice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kickoff Saturday | Watch the reveal, read the manual, argue about scoring | Opinions are welcome. Invented rules are not. |
| Week 1 | Musts, wants, prototypes on the table | The robot is not supposed to exist yet. |
| Weeks 2–3 | Pick mechanisms, prove them in wood or PLA, lock a frame perimeter | A failed prototype is the work, not a delay. |
| Weeks 3–4 | Cut, assemble, wire the competition robot | The shop gets loud. Bumper build starts only after the perimeter is real. |
| Weeks 5–6 | One robot, full match cycles, drivers, spares, freeze | A new idea that is not on the robot yet is probably too late. |

A deeper schedule, including shop nights and who owns each subsystem, is a later module. If this table and that later schedule disagree, the later schedule is the one you follow.

---

## What new members should expect

* **More hours.** After-school shop nights and some weekends. Schoolwork still comes first. Tell a lead early if you cannot make a night. Disappearing is the failure mode, not a conflict.
* **A subteam, not a free roam.** Mechanical, electrical, programming, design, strategy, and business all run at once. You will be placed. You can ask to try another area after you have been useful in the first one.
* **Standing around is normal for a day, not for a week.** If you are unassigned, ask a subteam lead for a job. Good first jobs: shop cleanup, part sorting, bumper help, watching a prototype test and writing down what failed, carrying a battery the right way.
* **The robot will look unfinished.** Week 2 is supposed to be ugly. Do not judge the season by the first prototype.
* **Mentors decide tool access.** Being present does not clear you on the mill, the CNC, or enabling the robot.
* **The game will feel obvious, then stop being obvious.** Read the manual section your subsystem touches. Highlight videos skip the fouls.

---

## What a successful season looks like from the shop floor

Success is not a secret mechanism. It is a robot that plays the game the team chose, with drivers who have already run that cycle, and a pit that can fix the failures found in week 5.

New members help that happen by showing up, staying on the task they were given, and reporting a problem before it becomes a missed night. Later modules cover strategy, manufacturing, and pit roles. This one only asks you to know the shape of January.
