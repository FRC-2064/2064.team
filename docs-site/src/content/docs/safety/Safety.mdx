**Objective:** Know the shop rules that apply before you pick up a tool, and know what to do in the first minute of a fire, an injury, or a leaking battery.

:::danger[STOP]
This page is the general credential. It does not sign you off on the CNC, lathe, mill, or enabling the robot. If you have not passed the safety quiz, you do not have clearance for the build area.
:::

The Panther Project shop is a shared workspace, not a garage. Food and drinks stay out. If you are unsure whether a tool is allowed for you, it is not.

## The three rules that fail people first

:::caution
**On before you start**
* **Glasses.** ANSI-rated safety glasses on whenever you are in the shop, the pit, or on a practice field. Prescription glasses need side shields or rated covers. Put them on before you approach a running machine.
* **Shoes.** Closed-toe and substantial. No Crocs, sandals, or flip-flops. A battery or a robot frame will find the open toe.
* **Hair and clothes.** Hair tied back. No dangling hoodie strings, loose jewelry, or baggy sleeves. Gloves are not allowed on rotating tools. They catch.
:::

A mentor can send you out of the area for any of the three. That is the rule working.

## Who does what

| Role | The job |
| --- | --- |
| Safety Captain | Inspections in the shop and the pit, emergency steps, calling out skipped PPE |
| Member | Read this page, pass the quiz, stop a teammate who is about to skip a rule, tell the Safety Captain when something breaks or someone is hurt |

Reporting is not tattling. A missing guard or a cracked battery case is how the next person gets hurt.

## Before you use a tool

* Area is clear, lit, and not a tripping path. Cords are not across the walkway.
* The tool is the right one, and it is not tagged out.
* Guards are on. You do not remove a guard to "just finish this cut."
* Both hands stay on a power tool. You do not carry it by the cord.
* Hand tools cut away from your body. A loose or splintered handle means the tool is out of service.
* Broken tool: unplug it or bleed the air, tag it "Do Not Use," and tell a mentor. That is lockout. Do not put it back because you need it.

## Soldering and robot power

* Lead-free solder only. Iron stays in the holder. Glasses on. Ventilation on. Wash your hands when you stop.
* Do not work on a live robot unless a mentor is on that job with you. Check the cord before you plug a strip in. Do not daisy-chain strips until the breaker trips.
* Enabling the robot is a separate procedure. Disabled, people clear, mentor present.

## Batteries

A competition battery is sulfuric acid in a plastic case. A swollen, cracked, or leaking case does not go on the robot.

If it is leaking:

1. Tell a mentor. Do not carry it against your clothes.
2. Baking soda on the wet surfaces to neutralize the acid.
3. Acid-resistant gloves and glasses, then a non-metal leak-proof container.
4. Skin contact means rinse and medical attention. Do not wait to see if it burns.

## If someone is hurt, or the building has to empty

* First aid kit is in the shop: bandages, gauze, tape, ointment, burn gel, alcohol pads, tweezers, ice packs, gloves. Restock what you use. Tell the Safety Captain if something is gone.
* Fire extinguishers are in the closet and outside the robotics room. Use one only if you have been trained and the exit is still behind you. Otherwise leave.
* Evacuation exits are near the Paint Room and near Mr. Napoli's office. Go to the meeting point outside. Do not go back in for a phone or a robot until a mentor clears it.
* You should never be in the shop working alone, there is no nurse in the building, an injury that happens can be serious, and a mentor is the first call. 
* If you are alone, leave the area and get a mentor.
* Building custodians are the next to notify for non-emergencies, if there is an minor issue - and a mentor is not readily available. 
* In the event a mentor is injured, or an adult is not immediatley present during an accident - Only Call 911 for a real emergency. 

## Phishing is a shop problem too

A fake sponsor email or a bad attachment is how the team loses a drive laptop. Use SLAM before you click:

* **Sender.** Does the address match the real company, or a lookalike?
* **Links.** Hover. A button that says Onshape and a URL that is not Onshape is a no.
* **Attachments.** Unexpected file, especially a zip or an invoice, does not get opened.
* **Message.** Urgency and a threat are the bait. Ask a mentor in person.
