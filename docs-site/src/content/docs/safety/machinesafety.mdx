**Objective:** Work near shop machines without becoming the person who gets hit by one. This page is the general credential. It is not a sign-off to run the CNC, lathe, mill, or bandsaw.

:::note[Prerequisites]
- [x] **1.3.1 - General Safety** and the certification quiz. Glasses, shoes, and tied hair are already required.
:::

:::danger[STOP]
Watching a mentor run a machine does not clear you to run it. If your pathway later includes fabrication, that training is separate. Until then you do not start a spindle, change a bit, or bypass a guard.
:::

A basic member is still in the room while other people cut. Most machine injuries in a student shop are not the operator's first cut. They are a walker, a cord, or a conversation in the wrong place.

## Where you are allowed to stand

* Stay out of the marked area around a running machine. If there is no tape, stay out of arm's reach of the bit, blade, or chuck.
* Do not walk behind someone who is feeding material. Their path if the tool grabs is backward.
* Do not talk to the operator while the tool is in the cut. Wait until the spindle is stopped.
* Chips and offcuts go on the floor. Do not reach in to grab a part until the machine is off and the operator says it is clear.
* Headphones off near machines.

## What you can do without a machine sign-off

* Carry stock to a bench, not into a running cut.
* Hold a flashlight or a drawing if a mentor asks, from outside the danger zone.
* Sweep only after the machine is off and the operator has left the chip area. A broom in a spinning chuck is a projectile.
* Tag a machine that is already stopped and obviously damaged, then get a mentor. Do not try to fix it.

## What stays off until pathway training

* Starting, stopping, or jogging a machine someone else set up.
* Removing a guard, a fence, or a hold-down because it is in the way.
* Wearing gloves on a rotating tool. Gloves are for batteries and solvent, not for lathes and drill presses.
* Compressed air aimed at skin, or at someone else, to clean chips. Chips get driven into eyes. Brush or vacuum after the machine is stopped.
* Leaving a chuck key, a wrench, or a hex key in the machine. If it is in your hand, it is not in the spindle.

## If it goes wrong

* Do not grab the person or the stock. Hit the e-stop if you can do it without reaching into the machine. Then get a mentor.
* Eye injury, a deep cut, or a crush: 911, then the mentor, then the first-aid kit. Do not decide it can wait until the part is finished.
* A broken bit or a thrown part means the area stays clear until a mentor says the machine is safe. Curiosity is how the second person gets hit.

:::note
**Core value check**
Leaving the machine alone is a skill. The pathway training is where you learn to run it. This page is where you learn not to be in its way.
:::
